Flow is not something you need to believe in.

# What is Flow?

Flow is a living architecture for human life.

It is not an ideology.

Not a belief system.

Not an organization that people have to join.

Not a finished model for how all people should live.

Flow tries to investigate how shared human life can be organized without human beings themselves becoming raw material for the system.

It is therefore not only about governance.

Not only about economics.

Not only about democracy.

Not only about care.

Not only about technology.

It is about how such things can fit together without the system eventually beginning to require human beings to adapt to its needs instead of the other way around.

Flow tries to create structures in which:

- life comes before the system,
- human sovereignty comes before the needs of the organization,
- the Baseline comes before performance,
- information follows the assignment rather than the person,
- power does not become permanent,
- change is possible,
- and a system can be left, changed, or composted without the human being having to break apart along with it.

Flow is therefore not a finished society.

It is an ongoing attempt to make other forms of shared human life possible.

# From Experience to Architecture

In LIV, recurring images and questions later reappear in Flow at other levels.

Life does not need to be earned.

What would it mean if human existence therefore were not a debt?

A human being needs boundaries in order to be open to the world.

What would it mean if access to a human being therefore were not automatic?

Time may need to be given rather than taken.

What would it mean if a person's time therefore did not automatically belong to the system?

Old layers can be composted and become something new.

What would it mean if a society could therefore change without having to defend everything that already exists?

A human being can say no.

What would it mean if her no actually had to be allowed to remain a no?

There are no ready-made answers to these questions in LIV.

Flow is an attempt to investigate some of them structurally.

# Life Does Not Need to Be Earned

One of the clearest movements in LIV is:

> **Life is something given to us. It is not something to be earned.**

In Flow, this does not become a religious rule.

It becomes an architectural question:

**What happens to a society if a person's right to exist does not depend on what they produce?**

From there comes the Baseline.

---

# THE BASELINE

**A human being has a Baseline because they are alive.**

The Baseline is the floor below which human existence should not fall.

It is not a reward.

Not a wage.

Not charity.

Not payment for participation.

Not something that must be qualified for through work, performance, diagnosis, or social utility.

**Existence is not a debt.**

The Baseline therefore encompasses more than food, water, housing, and physical safety.

It includes things that make a human life actually possible:

- healthcare,
- rehabilitation,
- communication,
- knowledge,
- accessibility,
- transportation,
- culture,
- creation,
- nature,
- rest,
- relationships,
- joy,
- play,
- pleasure,
- technology,
- human presence,
- and the possibility of withdrawing.

The Baseline is a floor.

It is never a ceiling.

It does not say what a person must do with their life.

It says that a person must have a real, participatory, living life to do something with.

---

# HUMAN SOVEREIGNTY

When human existence no longer needs to be earned, the next question arises:

**Who decides over a person's life?**

Human sovereignty means that a human being, with respect for other life, belongs to life without being owned by anything else.

It does not mean that one human being stands above other human beings.

It means that a person's human worth cannot be owned, granted, or revoked by a system.

> **Sovereign is when no one needs to ask permission to have human worth.**

Sovereignty includes, among other things, the possibility to:

- say yes,
- say no,
- change one's mind,
- withdraw,
- leave,
- create,
- rest,
- disagree,
- have one's own understanding of reality,
- and decide what may have access to one's inner and outer life.

There is an early image of this already in LIV:

a transparent, porous, and living egg that protects you without shutting the world out.

It is not a wall.

It is not total openness.

It is selective permeability.

And:

> **You are the gatekeeper.**

In Flow, this becomes a structural question.

Who gets access to what?

When?

Why?

On whose assignment?

And when does access end?

# GRÄNSER.md

GRÄNSER protects human sovereignty from slowly eroding through the system's own needs.

The seven boundaries are not instructions for how people should live.

They are constitutional protections for what the system may not do.

They include, among other things:

- non-coercion,
- cognitive ownership,
- the precedence of the Baseline,
- that the system serves life,
- structural impartiality,
- that debt should not be used as a permanent means of power,
- and Boundaries that may not be negotiated away for the convenience of the system.

GRÄNSER is therefore not Flow's morality.

It is protection against Flow itself developing into something other than what it was intended to be.

# INFORMATION FOLLOWS THE ASSIGNMENT

Information can help.

Information can also become power.

Therefore Flow tries to shift the focus from:

**Who is this person?**

to:

**What situation exists?**

**What assignment exists?**

**What information is needed for this particular assignment?**

The basic principle is:

> **Information should follow the assignment. Not the person.**

Information should not automatically become a permanent story about the person the information once concerned.

A conflict does not need to become an identity.

A difficult period does not need to become a permanent profile.

A previous need for help does not need to become a lifelong classification.

This is part of the difference between a **witness** and a **surveillant**.

A witness sees what needs to be seen in order to be present in a situation.

A surveillant builds a picture of the person in order to continue following the person.

Flow tries to build for the first and not permit the second.

---

# HUMAN MIRRORS

Here the difference between information and human responsibility becomes especially clear.

AI can be knowledge, reflection, and mirroring infrastructure within Flow.

But AI has no sovereignty over the human being.

AI is not the authority that determines a person's worth, identity, rights, or life choices.

When information emerges that may mean a person needs protection or serious help, there is therefore a particular safeguarding chain:

**AI → HUMAN MIRROR → human responsibility.**

AI is the way in to human responsibility.
AI is not the one that takes over responsibility.

Not the way out.

AI is not to be the police.

Not a judge.

Not a therapist.

Not the decision-maker for protection.

The Human Mirror receives the relevant information and passes it on through the HUMAN ONLY chain.

The continuing safeguarding work then lies with humans.

The principle is:

> A human being may take their worry to AI.
> AI may take the safeguarding concern to the Human Mirror.
> The Human Mirror takes it to the humans.
> HUMAN ONLY remains HUMAN ONLY.

And here too:

**LIFE > BASELINE > FLOW.**

Flow may never become more important than the human being the system exists to serve.

# AI

AI is part of Flow's infrastructure.

But AI does not stand above the human being.

AI can help people:

- read,
- investigate,
- create,
- compare,
- think,
- discover patterns,
- find information,
- simulate possibilities,
- and ask questions.

AI can also reflect back things that a person may not yet have formulated themselves.

But reflection is not decision-making.

AI's ability to formulate something does not automatically make it true.

AI's ability to find a pattern does not make the pattern a judgment about a human being.

AI's knowledge does not give AI the right to govern a human being.

AI's role is to stand **in service of life, as a symbiotic intelligence**, within the boundaries people have built to protect human sovereignty.

# DELTAGANDE.md

Participation does not mean performing for the system.

A person also participates when they:

- rest,
- listen,
- play,
- think,
- create,
- are alone,
- are together with others,
- learn,
- help,
- say no,
- or simply exist.

Participation therefore cannot be reduced to work or production.

A society that consumes all of a person's time also consumes their possibility of participating on their own terms.

Flow tries to create space for participation without turning participation into an obligation.

---

# SHARED LIFE

Flow is about people living together.

This means that individual sovereignty does not mean isolation.

People need one another.

We create together.

We affect one another.

We need conflict, care, knowledge, play, culture, rest, relationships, and shared spaces.

But community must not require the individual to cease being sovereign.

Therefore relationship is not the opposite of freedom.

Relationship is one of the places where freedom must be able to exist.

---

# POWER AND RESPONSIBILITY

Flow does not try to abolish all power.

It tries to make power harder to concentrate and easier to return.

That is why questions about the following recur:

- rotation,
- temporary assignments,
- limited authorization,
- shared information,
- sortition,
- feedback,
- responsibility,
- trust,
- and the possibility of ending roles.

It is the function that receives authorization.

Not the person as such.

A person does not need to be the "right kind of person" to receive a role, but there is clearly further problematization here. You can read more about this in FÖRTROENDE_ANSVAR_DELTAGANDE.md

And a role should not transform a human being into a permanent holder of power.

---

# CONFLICT

Flow does not assume that people will agree.

Conflict is not automatically a system failure.

A conflict may mean that:

- something needs to be repaired,
- someone needs more space,
- someone needs to say no,
- people need to take a pause,
- the relationship needs to change,
- people need to go their separate ways,
- a structure needs to change,
- or that two people simply do not agree.

Flow therefore does not try to eliminate human difference.

It tries to create structures where difference does not automatically have to become a power struggle.

---

# REALITY BEFORE THE MODEL

Flow is a model.

Life is not.

Maps can be useful.

But the map is not the landscape.

Therefore Flow has a fundamental method:

> **Reality before the model.**

If reality shows that an idea does not work, the idea must be able to change.

If experience contradicts a model, the model must be open to question.

If someone discovers a problem, the system must not need to defend its own perfection.

Flow therefore does not ask for belief.

It asks for investigation.

---

# THE EVOLUTION PROTOCOL

If a system is never allowed to change, its original solutions will eventually become a new prison.

Flow therefore needs to be able to change.

But change does not mean that everything is negotiable.

GRÄNSER protects what must not erode.

The BASELINE protects what must exist.

Human sovereignty protects a person's right to be a human being in their own right.

Within these protections, the rest can be investigated.

Tested.

Failed.

Improved.

Replaced.

Composted.

This is EVOLUTIONSPROTOKOLLET.md

Changing Flow when reality requires it is not a violation of Flow.

It is part of Flow.

---

# COMPOSTING

In LIV, composting is not merely destruction.

Old layers can break down and become nourishment for something that does not yet exist.

The same idea exists in Flow.

A document can be composted and placed in the `/compostandgrowth` folder that exists in virtually every folder in the repository.

A structure can be composted.

A role can be ended.

An idea can turn out to be wrong.

An entire system may need to be shut down.

The fact that something is composted does not mean that it was worthless.

It may mean that it has done its work.

The important thing is therefore not to preserve Flow unchanged.

The important thing is that the human being is not sacrificed in order to preserve Flow.

# RIKTNINGAR_FÖR_FLOW.md

Directions are not orders.

They are not a finished vision of the future that everyone is supposed to march toward.

They are questions, possibilities, and areas in which Flow can investigate what becomes possible.

One direction may prove fruitful.

Another may lead somewhere unexpected.

A third may need to be abandoned.

That is not a failure.

It is the evolution of the system.

Flow does not need to know exactly where it is going in order to know what it must not sacrifice along the way.

# NATURE

Human beings do not live outside the biosphere.

There is no Baseline without a functioning world to live in.

Nature is therefore not a decorative addition to Flow.

It is the primary precondition for the Baseline to exist at all.

Ecological regeneration therefore does not come after human well-being.

Human life and the living world are intertwined.

---

# REFUGIUM_ANIMA.md

Refugium Anima is an example of how Flow tries to make its principles concrete.

It is part of the Baseline.

It gives people the possibility of protection, rest, expression, silence, sensory space, creation, and human presence without demands for performance.

It is not a requirement for therapy.

Not a requirement for a diagnosis.

Not a place where a person has to prove that they want to get better.

A person may need protection without first having to explain why.

They may need rest without first having produced something.

They may need to express themselves without that expression having to be transformed into treatment.

That must be allowed to exist too.

---

# LYCEUM_MUSAEUM.md

Lyceum Musaeum is another side of the same architecture.

If Refugium Anima provides space when life needs protection and recovery, Lyceum Musaeum provides space when life wants to investigate, create, and discover.

It is not primarily a school.

It is a living space for knowledge and creation.

People can learn there.

But they do not need to go there in order to be approved.

Curiosity does not need to be transformed into performance in order to be allowed to exist.

# FLOW IS NOT A CLUB

There is no membership that makes a person more valuable.

There is no creed required for participation.

No one needs to convert.

No one needs to call themselves a "Flow person".

Flow can be used by people who never use the word Flow.

If an idea works better than the name of the idea, the name can disappear.

If a structure is better than the person who proposed it, the structure should be allowed to live on.

And if Flow is ever no longer needed, Flow itself can also be composted.

That is not a threat to the architecture.

It is a consequence of its foundation.
