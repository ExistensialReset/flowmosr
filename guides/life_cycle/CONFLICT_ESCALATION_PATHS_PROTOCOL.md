# Conflict Escalation Paths Protocol (CEPP) 

## Purpose 

This protocol describes how Flow responds when conflict becomes persistent, destructive, structurally significant, or incompatible with continued shared operation. The purpose is not to punish conflict. Conflict is information. The purpose is to determine whether the present configuration can be repaired, transformed, paused, forked, or ended. 

--- 

## 1. Core Principle 

Conflict does not automatically mean failure. Conflict may reveal: 
- unmet needs 
- incompatible expectations 
- structural pressure 
- inaccessible conditions 
- unclear boundaries 
- accumulated grief 
- resource scarcity 
- communication breakdown 
- unresolved power differences 
- incompatible directions 

The first response is therefore not escalation. It is understanding. 

---

## 2. Baseline Comes First 

The governing hierarchy is: ```text
Life → Baseline → Flow 

Baseline must remain protected regardless of the state of the conflict.

No conflict protocol may remove a person's unconditional human worth or access to necessary Baseline conditions.

If Flow cannot continue without compromising Baseline, Flow must yield.

## 3. L × S × I is Not an Escalation Score

L × S × I may be used as a qualitative reflection compass:

L = Lugn 
S = Spontanitet 
I = Inkännande 

Participants may ask:

Is there enough calm to remain present?

Is there enough spontaneity to imagine alternatives?

Is there enough attunement to hear one another?

These questions are reflective.

They are not numerical measurements.

This protocol does not use:

L × S × I < 0.3 L × S × I < 0.6 L × S × I for 60 days L × S × I for 6 months 

as automatic escalation thresholds.

There is no numerical L × S × I trigger for declaring a conflict terminal.

## 4. The Escalation Logic

The actual escalation sequence is:

Conflict ↓ Recognition ↓ 
Pause ↓ 
Listening ↓ 
Investigation ↓ 
Repair attempt ↓ 
Structural assessment ↓ 
Renewal / 
transformation / 
fork / 
ending 

Escalation means increasing the depth of human attention, not increasing punishment.

## 5. Level 0 — Ordinary Conflict

Conflict is ordinary when people can still:

remain in dialogue

repair after rupture

acknowledge one another's experience

respect boundaries

take pauses

return voluntarily

distinguish disagreement from attack

Response:

listen slow down name the pattern repair continue 

No formal escalation is required.

## 6. Level 1 — Recurring Conflict

Conflict becomes significant when a similar pattern repeatedly returns.

Possible indicators include:

the same trigger appearing repeatedly

repeated failed repair

increasing exhaustion

people losing trust in the process

recurring feelings of not being heard

increasing difficulty returning after conflict

structural conditions repeatedly recreating the conflict

These are observations, not scores.

Response:

pause document the pattern where appropriate invite reflection identify structural contributors attempt repair 

AI may assist with summarization or pattern recognition, but human interpretation remains primary.

## 7. Level 2 — Persistent Structural Conflict

Conflict becomes structurally significant when the same failure persists despite good-faith repair attempts.

Questions include:

Is the structure itself producing the conflict?

Are resources distributed in a way that repeatedly creates pressure?

Are boundaries unclear or incompatible?

Is participation genuinely voluntary?

Is accessibility adequate?

Is there a power imbalance?

Are people repeatedly being asked to adapt to structural failure?

Has the present configuration become unsustainable?

The answer must not be:

"The people need to become better adapted."

The system must investigate itself.

## 8. Reflection Pause

A Reflection Pause may be initiated when:

multiple people independently sense significant drift

the conflict repeatedly prevents meaningful dialogue

continued activity would worsen the situation

structural questions cannot be answered while normal activity continues

The pause should:

protect Baseline

suspend non-essential decisions where appropriate

create space for listening

allow independent reflection

make relevant structural conditions visible

prevent retaliation for raising concerns

A Reflection Pause is not punishment.

It is not exclusion.

It is not a verdict.

## 9. The Body and Somatic Information

Somatic experience can be important evidence.

People may report:

tension

exhaustion

fear

relief

shutdown

agitation

calm

increased capacity

loss of capacity

These experiences should be taken seriously.

They should not be universalized.

One person's bodily response is not automatically evidence of another person's intention.

Somatic information is one witness among several.

## 10. Repair

Repair may involve:

changing the environment

changing responsibilities

changing communication structures

introducing more recovery time

clarifying boundaries

changing meeting formats

improving accessibility

redistributing resources

bringing in an independent facilitator

taking temporary distance

creating a new agreement

Repair must address causes.

It should not merely demand improved tolerance.

## 11. When Repair Is Not Working

Repair may be considered unsuccessful when:

the same destructive pattern continues

meaningful trust cannot be restored

boundaries remain incompatible

structural conditions cannot be repaired

participation no longer feels voluntary

continued togetherness requires coercion

people repeatedly become less safe through the process

No numerical score is required to establish this.

The relevant evidence is the lived and structural reality.

## 12. Fork, Don't Fight

Not every incompatible configuration needs to become a winner-and-loser conflict.

Where possible:

Fork, don't fight. 

A fork may mean:

separate Circles

different projects

different rhythms

different governance structures

independent Nodes

different priorities

temporary or permanent separation

A fork is not failure.

Sometimes separation is the most respectful form of continuation.

## 13. Ending a Configuration

A configuration may end when:

repair has genuinely been attempted

incompatibility remains

continuation causes significant harm

Baseline cannot be protected within the configuration

coercion would otherwise be required

the participants choose to separate

Ending does not mean:

bad people 

It means:

this configuration no longer works 

Leaving is not betrayal.

Ending is not punishment.

Dissolution is not necessarily failure.

## 14. Terminal Conflict

"Terminal" should describe a configuration, not a person.

A conflict is terminal when there is no credible, voluntary, and non-destructive path to continue the current configuration.

This determination must be based on:

lived experience

observed patterns

structural conditions

boundary compatibility

available repair possibilities

Baseline protection

human judgment

It must never be determined by an L × S × I score.

## 15. Power and Coercion

If continued operation requires:

threats

punishment

forced participation

deprivation

surveillance

humiliation

coercive compliance

then the system must stop and reassess.

A system that requires coercion to remain intact has already revealed a structural failure.

## 16. AI Role in Conflict

AI may:

summarize statements

identify recurring themes

surface contradictions

suggest reflection questions

help separate observation from interpretation

help document agreements

identify missing perspectives

AI may not:

determine guilt

assign L × S × I scores

declare a person dysfunctional

declare a person unsafe without human assessment

force a pause

force reconciliation

force separation

determine the final outcome

AI remains advisory.

Human judgment remains primary.

## 17. Documentation

Documentation should be:

proportionate

relevant

transparent

privacy-conscious

limited to what is necessary

Do not create permanent behavioural profiles.

Do not publish intimate personal information merely because a conflict occurred.

Where public accountability is necessary, use the minimum information required.

Cognitive ownership remains with the person.

## 18. Evidence Hierarchy

When assessing persistent conflict, consider:

First: lived experience

What are people actually experiencing?

Second: observable patterns

What keeps happening?

Third: structural conditions

What in the environment may be producing or reinforcing the pattern?

Fourth: boundaries

Are the relevant boundaries clear and respected?

Fifth: repair capacity

Can the configuration change enough to become sustainable?

Sixth: human judgment

What do the people involved conclude after reflection?

No single signal is automatically decisive.

## 19. The Non-Individualization Rule

A central safeguard is:

The system must not put responsibility for structural dysfunction onto the individual and then call individual adaptation the solution.

Therefore:

structural failure ↓ structural investigation ↓ structural repair 

not:

structural failure ↓ identify the "problem person" ↓ train the person to tolerate the failure 

This principle applies to:

accessibility

resources

communication structures

governance

workload

conflict

participation

technology

AI systems

## 21. Core Principle

Conflict is information.

A pause is not punishment.

A pattern is not a person.

A score is not a human being.

Structural failure must be investigated structurally.

Forking is sometimes healthier than fighting.

Ending is allowed.

Baseline comes first.

If Flow requires coercion to survive, Flow must yield.
