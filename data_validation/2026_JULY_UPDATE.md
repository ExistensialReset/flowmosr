## MINOR UPDATE

**Fossil fuel subsidies.** IMF's most recent update (December 2025, "Underpriced and Overused: Fossil Fuel Subsidies Data 2025 Update," covering 2024 data) puts total subsidies — explicit + implicit — at $7.4 trillion, or 6.4% of global GDP (explicit $725B / 0.6%, implicit $6.7T / 5.8%). The dollar figure is a touch higher than the $7T cited; the GDP share is lower (7.1%→6.4%) because explicit subsidies have fallen back from their war-driven 2022 peak while global GDP has grown. Worth a number update; doesn't change the conclusion.

## MATERIAL UPDATES

### CO2 baseline is stale

ECOLOGICAL_REGENERATION_v2.md's entire CO2 trajectory section starts from 420 ppm. Actual: the 2025 global annual average was 427.35 ppm (a record), and the UK Met Office's 2026 forecast puts the annual average around 429.4 ± 0.6 ppm, peaking near 432.2 ppm in May — which the Met Office notes is the highest atmospheric CO2 in over two million years. The logic of the section doesn't change (net emissions still positive against natural sinks; stabilization still ~15–25 years out; real reduction still 50–80), but it should be re-run from roughly 428 ppm, not 420.

### The peace dividend pool is bigger — and the trend is the wrong direction

PEACE_DIVIDEND_v2.md cites $2.4T/year in military spending (SIPRI 2024). SIPRI's newest figures (released April 2026, covering 2025) put global military expenditure at $2.887 trillion — the 11th straight year of growth, 2.5% of global GDP (the highest share since 2009), up 41% over 2016–2025. Europe rose 14% and Asia-Oceania 8.1% in 2025 alone; the US has already approved over $1T for 2026, with a $1.5T proposal on the table for 2027. The $2.4T→$3.1T dividend range in section 7 should rebase off something closer to $2.9T — a bigger potential dividend, but attached to a trend that's accelerating, not plateauing.

### Conflict data — the finding most worth sitting with

PEACE_DIVIDEND_v2.md's baseline (section 1) is 56 active conflicts and ~120,000 deaths for 2024. UCDP's own released numbers:

| Year | Active state-based conflicts | Deaths (organised violence) |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 59 | — |
| 2024 | 61 | 187,000 |
| 2025 (released June 2026) | 65 — highest since UCDP records began in 1946 | 244,600 — highest since the Rwandan genocide, 1994 |

Both figures were already higher for 2024 than what the document cites, and 2025 is worse again on both counts. Interstate war — states fighting states, not civil wars — has doubled two years running: from 2 in 2023 to roughly 4 in 2024 to 8 in 2025, also a record since 1946 (includes Russia–Ukraine, Iran–Israel, India–Pakistan, Israel–Syria).

The part worth sitting with, in the spirit of this document's own method: PEACE_DIVIDEND_v2.md's own section 3 estimates Baseline security has its *smallest* effect on exactly the conflict types now driving the surge — territorial/interstate disputes (10–20% impact) and ideological conflict (5–15%) — versus the resource conflicts it's built to address (60–80%). The empirical trend since February is running opposite to the "realistic projection," and it's concentrated in the category the mechanism is weakest against. That doesn't break the argument — Baseline was never claimed to touch interstate war much. But it does mean the 15–25 year timeline and 29–43% reduction estimate are now being measured against a worse starting point than they were five months ago, and the worsening is happening in the part of the picture Baseline can't reach.

No clean explanation for why the document's original 56/~120,000 don't match UCDP's own 2024 figures of 61/187,000 — could be a different source, a different data vintage, or a narrower definition (e.g. battle deaths only, excluding one-sided violence against civilians). Worth tracing back to whatever the original source was, per the Replication Test.
