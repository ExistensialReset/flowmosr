## 1. PURPOSE & DESIGN PRINCIPLES

This document defines a **self‑sufficient urban node** that produces food, energy, water, and housing for 500 people – without using money, according to the Flow Node concept. All resources are distributed automatically via the **Baseline**.

**Key changes from previous versions:**
- No spirulina (taste and energy reasons)
- Focus on pea shoots, mushrooms, lettuce, tomatoes, beans/lentils
- Energy consumption halved
- Regional adaptation of solar irradiance and material costs

## 3. CROP MIX & PROTEIN PRODUCTION

### 3.1 Why These Crops?

- **Pea shoots** – sweet, 25% protein, fast harvest (7–10 days), low energy.
- **Oyster / shiitake mushrooms** – umami, fibre, 3.3% protein, very energy‑efficient.
- **Lettuce & leafy greens** – base, vitamins, minimal energy.
- **Tomatoes** – variety, nutritious, popular.
- **Beans/lentils** – storable, 25% protein, grown in substrate (soil/compost) indoors.

**No spirulina** – no fishy/grassy taste.

### 4.3 Paths to 100% Self‑Sufficiency

- **Option A (expanded solar):** Install solar panels on facades, light shafts, and any available parking area. In a dense urban area, you might reach ~800 kW peak → 720,000 kWh/yr (Sweden) → coverage 48%.
- **Option B (energy storage + purchase from surplus nodes):** Connect several urban nodes in an energy grid – nodes in sunnier regions (Southern Europe) produce 1,500 kWh/kW, giving 1,200,000 kWh for the same roof area → coverage 67%.
- **Option C (hybrid):** Place a 2‑hectare solar field just outside the city (still within the region) – produces ~2,400,000 kWh/yr, covering the entire need with surplus.

**Recommendation:** In Nordic regions, the most cost‑effective solution is to combine rooftop solar, biogas, and a small ground‑mounted solar array (0.5 ha) – owned by the node but not used for food production. This is still "urban" in the sense that all food comes from within buildings.

## 10. INTEGRATION WITH THE FLOW NODE CONCEPT

- **Baseline:** All food, energy, water, and housing are provided automatically – no applications, no bureaucracy.
- **Work:** Farming and maintenance tasks are shared voluntarily; the total time (8.8 h/person/year) is so small that almost everyone can contribute a little each week.
- **Regional exchange:** Surplus food (e.g., excess pea shoots) can be exchanged for other goods via the regional flow system (15% of production). Globally (5%), coffee, spices, and specialities can flow.
- **Quality:** Certified growers and peer review ensure food safety and system reliability.
