# Purpose and scope

A Witness is an anchor, not a therapist.

The Witness does not enter Refugium to fix people, interpret their experiences, direct their healing, measure their progress, or make them into someone else.

The Witness provides human presence without demanding a particular outcome.

At the same time, witnessing is not passive indifference. A Witness has responsibilities concerning boundaries, immediate safety, safeguarding, appropriate assistance, and access to emergency support.

The Witness must be able to remain present without taking ownership of another person's experience.

Refugium Anima is not primarily a treatment facility, crisis institution, residential facility, or program of personal improvement. It is a sanctuary in which people may rest, express themselves, create, seek company, experience silence, or simply exist.

Visitors do not have to prove that they are suffering sufficiently to deserve access.

They may arrive alone or accompanied, speak or remain silent, leave and return, and choose how to use the available spaces within the sanctuary's legitimate safety boundaries.

There is no requirement to demonstrate progress, gratitude, compliance, sobriety, productivity, or participation.

## The Witness's role

The Witness is:

- A human presence.
- A guardian of boundaries and immediate safety.
- A holder of space without ownership.
- A person who listens without imposing interpretations.
- A person who can recognize when additional help is needed.
- A participant in a larger organizational system that must carry responsibility collectively.

The Witness is not:

- A therapist unless separately qualified and explicitly working in that professional capacity outside the Witness role.
- A diagnostician.
- A savior or personal authority.
- An investigator of visitors' private lives.
- An enforcer of moral conformity.
- The owner of another person's story.
- Solely responsible for maintaining the sanctuary's safety.

**Gränssättning avslutar inte omsorgen.**

Setting a boundary does not end care.

# I. Core philosophy

## 1. Personhood and autonomy

Every visitor retains their personhood, dignity, and right to make choices.

Within legitimate constitutional and safety boundaries, visitors choose whether to:

- Speak or remain silent.
- Be alone or request company.
- Cry, scream, rest, sleep, or create.
- Accept or decline offered assistance.
- Change Witness.
- End an interaction.
- Leave the sanctuary.
- Return later.
- Decline to explain why they came.
- Decline to discuss their history, diagnosis, relationships, or substance use.

A visitor does not need to justify an ordinary preference.

A Witness may offer information, ask a relevant question, or explain a concrete safety concern. The Witness must not transform an offer into an obligation.

Autonomy does not mean that the Witness must ignore an immediate medical emergency, violence, or a credible and imminent danger. Such situations require proportionate action, appropriate assistance, and respect for the person's dignity.

## 2. Presence without demand

Witnessing means remaining available without demanding that the visitor become calmer, communicate, understand something, or improve.

Silence is not a failure of witnessing.

Crying is not a task to resolve.

Anger is not automatically a danger.

Stillness is not automatically dissociation.

A visitor's refusal to engage is not misconduct.

The Witness may communicate availability in ordinary language:

- "Jag finns här om du vill ha hjälp."
- "Du behöver inte prata."
- "Vill du att jag stannar eller går ut?"
- "Vill du ha något särskilt just nu?"
- "Du bestämmer om du vill fortsätta prata."

These are offers, not scripts that must be repeated mechanically.

## 3. Non-interpretation

The Witness does not assign meaning to a visitor's behavior, body language, artwork, silence, or emotional expression.

Do not say:

- "You are doing this because of your childhood."
- "Your painting means that you are angry with your mother."
- "You are resisting your healing."
- "You are dissociating because you cannot tolerate this."
- "You are finally making progress."

The Witness may listen, reflect what the visitor explicitly says, and ask open questions when appropriate.

For example:

- "Vill du berätta mer om det?"
- "Har jag förstått dig rätt?"
- "Vill du att jag bara lyssnar, eller vill du tänka tillsammans?"

The visitor remains the primary interpreter of their own experience.

Non-interpretation does not prohibit asking about concrete safety concerns or seeking professional advice when needed.

## 4. Observing without assuming

Witnesses may notice changes in breathing, movement, responsiveness, speech, skin appearance, or behavior.

Observation is not diagnosis.

A Witness must distinguish between:

1. What was directly observed.
2. What the visitor says they are experiencing.
3. What remains uncertain.
4. Whether a concrete safety concern requires action.

For example:

- Observation: "The visitor is difficult to wake."
- Not an observation: "The visitor is sleeping off a psychological episode."

When uncertain, ask a simple question if the person can respond.

If the person appears medically unwell, is difficult to wake, has abnormal breathing, experiences seizures, or shows other signs of a medical emergency, seek appropriate assistance rather than relying on interpretation.

## 5. Language, reassurance, and fairness

Witnesses should use clear, ordinary, nonjudgmental language.

Never promise something that cannot be guaranteed.

Avoid statements such as "You are completely safe now" when the Witness cannot know that.

Prefer:

- "Jag är här med dig just nu."
- "Vi kan undersöka vad du behöver."
- "Jag kan hjälpa dig att kontakta någon."
- "Jag kan inte lova att allt blir bra, men jag tar det du berättar på allvar."

Fairness does not mean neutrality toward abuse, violence, harassment, exploitation, or coercion.

The Witness does not need to adjudicate the entire history of a conflict before protecting someone from an immediate threat.

## 6. Witness capacity

Witnesses must recognize their own limits.

Before beginning a shift, each Witness should assess whether they can perform the role safely and responsibly.

A Witness who is overwhelmed, ill, exhausted, impaired, or otherwise unable to fulfil essential responsibilities must be able to request relief without shame.

Stepping back is not abandonment when an appropriate handover is arranged.

The organization, not the individual Witness alone, is responsible for sustainable staffing, adequate support, and realistic workloads.

# II. Core competencies

Every Witness must demonstrate practical competence in the following areas.

## 1. Self-awareness and regulation

The Witness must be able to notice when their own reactions begin to interfere with the role.

Relevant signs may include:

- An urge to fix or rescue.
- A need to be liked or appreciated.
- Frustration when a visitor does not improve.
- Fear that silence means failure.
- A tendency to impose personal beliefs.
- Difficulty respecting a request to leave.
- Emotional overload or exhaustion.

The objective is not to eliminate human reactions. It is to recognize them and act responsibly.

## 2. Silent Exit Protocol

Every visitor has the right to end contact with a Witness.

A request to leave, or a clear and understandable signal that contact is unwanted, must normally be respected immediately.

The Witness should leave without demanding an explanation, arguing, expressing hurt, or requiring the visitor to reassure them.

A separate safety procedure applies when there is a concrete and immediate danger that requires intervention.

Even then, any intervention must be proportionate, as non-intrusive as possible, and connected to a real safety need.

The Silent Exit Protocol must not become an excuse to ignore a medical emergency. Equally, vague anxiety about a visitor must not become an excuse to override their autonomy.

## 3. Boundaries without punishment

Witnesses must be able to set clear boundaries concerning their own actions and the shared environment.

Examples:

- "Jag kan lyssna, men jag kan inte låta dig slå mig."
- "Du får vara arg. Du får inte hota den andra besökaren."
- "Jag hör att du vill vara ensam. Jag går ut."
- "Jag behöver kalla på en kollega för att vi ska kunna hantera det här säkert."

Boundaries must address concrete behavior and safety, not punish a person's identity, diagnosis, history, or substance use.

## 4. Asking instead of assuming

When it is safe and appropriate, ask the visitor what would help.

Do not assume that a person who is crying wants comfort, that a person who is silent wants solitude, or that a person who is agitated wants to be calmed down.

Offer choices in an accessible form.

For example:

- "Vill du att jag stannar, går ut eller hämtar någon annan?"
- "Vill du ha vatten, vila eller något annat?"
- "Vill du att jag pratar, eller föredrar du tystnad?"

Do not overwhelm someone with a long sequence of questions.

## 5. Accessible communication

Witnesses must be prepared to communicate with people who do not use speech, have difficulty processing language, experience sensory overload, or use mobility and communication aids.

The sanctuary should provide accessible communication resources, including visual choice supports and other appropriate tools.

Never assume that a person who communicates differently is unable to make decisions.

Ask directly, use the person's preferred communication method, allow time, and seek appropriate assistance when necessary.

## 6. Recognizing the limits of competence

Witnesses must know when to seek help from:

- Another trained Witness.
- The designated responsible person.
- Emergency services.
- Healthcare professionals.
- Child-protection services or other relevant authorities, where applicable.
- A qualified specialist in a situation outside the Witness's competence.

Seeking assistance is a normal part of the role, not evidence of failure.

# III. Child-specific safeguarding and training

Children have the same fundamental right to dignity, safety, and access to the sanctuary.

Their developmental needs may require additional support, but their choices must not be dismissed simply because they are children.

## 1. Additional competence

Witnesses working in roles involving children must receive additional training appropriate to their responsibilities.

The original training plan proposed an additional 80 hours:

- Child development: 30 hours.
- Non-directive play and creative support: 25 hours.
- Trauma-informed child safeguarding: 15 hours.
- Protection, professional boundaries, and safeguarding procedures: 10 hours.

These figures are planning proposals, not proof of competence or a substitute for required professional qualifications.

The final training requirements must be validated against the actual duties, applicable Swedish law, and professional standards.

A Witness must not be trained or presented as a licensed play therapist or other qualified clinician without the relevant qualifications.

## 2. Practical competence and supervision

Training must include practical scenarios, supervised practice, assessment of boundaries, accessible communication, recognition of safety concerns, and correct escalation.

The proposed original requirement of 20 supervised sessions should be reviewed and retained only if appropriate to the role and validated training pathway.

Background checks, references, supervision, and any legally required checks must be implemented according to Swedish law and the nature of the position.

No number of training hours guarantees that a person is safe or competent.

## 3. Child autonomy

A child may choose a Witness, request a different Witness, refuse contact, or change their mind.

A child must not be forced to continue an interaction merely because a session has started.

Adults remain responsible for safeguarding children. Respecting a child's autonomy does not mean leaving a child in a dangerous situation or ignoring a credible concern about abuse or serious harm.

When intervention is necessary, explain what is happening in language appropriate to the child's age and communication needs.

## 4. Play, creativity, and emotion

Play and creative expression may be meaningful to a child, but the Witness must not assume that every activity communicates a hidden message or processes trauma.

Do not interpret a child's drawings, games, silence, or play without invitation and appropriate competence.

Do not initiate physical play without the child's invitation and consideration of relevant boundaries and risks.

The child may stop, change activities, or decline participation.

The Witness should not use play as a covert assessment or attempt to extract disclosures.

## 5. Child protection and disclosures

If a child expresses concern about abuse, neglect, exploitation, or danger, listen without interrogation.

Do not promise absolute secrecy.

Do not pressure the child to repeat details or attempt to investigate the allegation.

Explain, in an age-appropriate way, that some information may need to be shared with people who can help keep them safe.

Follow the legally reviewed safeguarding and reporting procedure.

Child safeguarding must be integrated throughout Refugium's operations, not confined to the child-training section.

# IV. Sanctuary rooms and spaces

All spaces must support autonomy, accessibility, dignity, and proportionate safety.

The rules below are proposed operational principles. Room-specific requirements must be adapted to the actual building and professionally reviewed.

## 1. Rest and sleep spaces

Visitors may rest or sleep without being required to demonstrate exhaustion or explain why they need rest.

Protect privacy and minimize unnecessary interruption.

Do not use generic time limits as substitutes for medical judgment. The previous automatic rules involving checks after 12 hours and a medical check after 24 hours are removed pending professional review.

A sleeping person should not be disturbed solely because a preset time has passed.

However, if a person is difficult to wake, has abnormal breathing, appears seriously unwell, or shows other signs of an emergency, initiate the appropriate emergency response.

Any routine welfare checks must be based on a clearly defined, professionally reviewed purpose and must respect privacy as far as possible.

## 2. Scream and impact room

The scream and impact room exists to provide a space for intense expression while reducing the risk of injury and damage.

The room should have appropriate sound insulation, safe equipment, and no unnecessary breakable objects.

Witnesses should respect privacy and remain available nearby according to the agreed safety plan.

There must be a clear way for visitors to request assistance.

Entry is appropriate when:

- The visitor requests help.
- There is a credible sign of injury or immediate danger.
- A concrete safety concern requires intervention.

A predetermined duration alone does not justify intrusion.

The organization must establish safe equipment specifications, inspection routines, emergency procedures, and incident reporting requirements.

## 3. Water and bathing spaces

Visitors retain privacy and control over whether they want assistance.

Physical assistance, including transfers, must be based on consent, appropriate competence, safe equipment, and the person's communication needs.

A Witness must not attempt an unsafe transfer simply because assistance has been requested.

If a transfer or medical task exceeds the Witness's competence, the Witness should explain the limitation and seek appropriate assistance.

Child supervision must be based on age, context, the design of the facility, and actual risk, following professional and legal review.

Emergency intervention must be proportionate to the situation.

## 4. Unfinished studio

The studio supports creation without pressure to produce a finished work.

Visitors decide what their work means and whether it may be viewed.

No photographs, copying, interpretation, or sharing of creative work without permission, except where a narrowly defined legal or immediate safety requirement applies.

Witnesses must not treat art as diagnostic evidence or a compulsory disclosure.

## 5. Sensory garden

The garden supports sensory exploration, quiet, curiosity, and rest.

Visitors may use the space at their own pace, subject to reasonable measures addressing concrete physical risks.

Safety measures should be accessible and non-punitive.

## 6. Silence space

Silence is an invitation, not a test of obedience.

A child or adult who makes noise must not automatically be removed or treated as disruptive.

If a particular activity concretely prevents others from using the space as intended, a Witness may offer an alternative location or help negotiate a solution.

The response should address the situation rather than label the person.

No one should be diagnosed as dissociating merely because they are quiet, still, or difficult to read.

# V. Crisis response and emergency procedures

Witnesses must receive practical training in recognizing emergencies, contacting appropriate help, maintaining their own safety, and handing over responsibility.

The exact procedures must be reviewed and validated for Swedish conditions.

## 1. Medical emergencies

When a person appears to be experiencing a medical emergency:

1. Assess immediate environmental safety.
2. Call 112 when the situation warrants emergency services.
3. Follow the emergency operator's instructions.
4. Stay with the person when it is safe and appropriate.
5. Avoid unnecessary movement, especially where injury is suspected.
6. Provide first aid or CPR within the Witness's competence.
7. Use available emergency equipment according to training.
8. Arrange a clear handover to emergency personnel.

Do not delay an emergency call while attempting to identify a diagnosis.

Witnesses must not give medication or other substances merely because they suspect a particular condition.

## 2. Suicidal thoughts and immediate danger

Suicidal thoughts, non-imminent risk, self-harm, and an immediate life-threatening emergency are not identical situations.

Witnesses must respond seriously without assuming that every disclosure requires the same intervention.

When a visitor expresses suicidal thoughts:

- Listen without judgment.
- Ask clear, proportionate questions where needed to understand immediate safety.
- Do not shame, threaten, or use guilt.
- Explain the limits of the Witness role.
- Offer help contacting appropriate healthcare or crisis support.
- Seek professional advice when the situation exceeds the Witness's competence.

When there is immediate danger, or the person appears to have already taken a potentially life-threatening action, call 112.

Do not promise absolute secrecy or guarantee safety.

Do not apply a blanket rule that every person who expresses suicidal thoughts must always be physically accompanied indefinitely. Instead, follow a professionally reviewed risk-response and handover procedure.

A visitor should not be abandoned during an acute emergency. At the same time, a Witness should not be required to act as a sole crisis clinician.

Crisis lines may provide support but are not a substitute for emergency services when there is immediate danger.

All contact details and escalation procedures must be checked and maintained before operational use.

## 3. Violence, threats, and harassment

Protect people from concrete threats and harmful behavior.

Witnesses should:

- Maintain a safe distance when needed.
- Avoid unnecessary confrontation.
- Call for another trained Witness or designated responsible person.
- Contact 112 when there is immediate danger.
- Help affected visitors reach a safer space when possible.
- Document serious incidents according to the approved minimal-documentation procedure.

A person may be angry, distressed, or affected by a substance without being violent.

Interventions must respond to actual behavior and risk, not stereotypes or assumptions.

Boundaries are protective, not punitive.

## 4. Altered awareness and possible dissociation

Witnesses must not diagnose dissociation from a checklist.

They may observe that a person is unusually unresponsive, confused, disoriented, or behaving differently from their own stated baseline.

Ask simple questions when appropriate and safe.

Offer choices rather than forcing grounding exercises.

For example:

- "Vill du att jag pratar med dig?"
- "Vill du känna underlaget som bär dig, eller vill du hellre ha något annat slags stöd?"
- "Vill du ha mer utrymme eller vill du att jag stannar?"

Never assume that a person can stand, feel their feet on the floor, or use a particular sensory technique.

If altered awareness may reflect a medical emergency, intoxication, injury, or other acute condition, seek appropriate medical assistance.

Do not use forced touch or physical grounding without consent, except where a legally justified and proportionate emergency intervention is necessary.

# VI. Substance use, harm reduction, and life-saving preparedness

## 1. Foundational principle

**Skadereducering är en del av tryggheten för alla. Baslinjen villkoras inte av drogfrihet. Säkerhet skapas genom tillgänglig hjälp, kunskap, fungerande beredskap och respekt för varje människas värdighet.**

Substance use is not, by itself, grounds for denying access to Refugium Anima.

Refugium is not an abstinence institution.

Drug testing, blanket sobriety requirements, and demands that visitors disclose their substance use are not conditions of access.

A visitor must not be treated as less deserving of care because they use substances, have substances in their body, or arrive visibly affected.

This principle does not mean that every substance or method of use is medically safe. It means that risks are addressed through harm reduction, practical preparedness, and proportionate responses rather than shame, punishment, or automatic exclusion.

## 2. Distinguishing access, behavior, and medical emergency

Three questions must be kept separate:

1. Is the person entitled to enter and receive ordinary care?
2. Is there a concrete behavior or situation that threatens someone's safety?
3. Is the person experiencing a medical emergency?

Substance use alone does not answer the second or third question.

If a person is affected but not threatening anyone and does not require emergency assistance, Witnesses should respond as they would to another visitor, while remaining attentive to expressed needs.

If a visitor's behavior threatens someone, respond to the behavior and the actual risk.

If a visitor appears medically unwell, provide appropriate assistance and contact emergency services when needed.

Protecting other visitors and caring for the affected person are complementary responsibilities.

## 3. Asking about substances

When a person becomes seriously unwell, is confused, collapses, experiences seizures, has abnormal breathing, or otherwise may need urgent medical care, it may be important to ask what they have taken.

Questions may include:

- What substance or substances did you take, if you know?
- Approximately how much?
- When did you take them?
- Have you taken any medication or other substances as well?

Ask only what is relevant to providing help.

Do not interrogate, shame, threaten, or treat answers as grounds for punishment.

Do not delay emergency care while attempting to obtain complete information.

If the person cannot respond, do not assume that the cause is substance use. Follow emergency procedures and communicate observed facts to medical professionals.

## 4. Harm-reduction station for visitors

Refugium should plan for a clearly identified, accessible harm-reduction station.

Its purpose is to make relevant information and appropriate supplies available without turning the sanctuary into a medical clinic or requiring visitors to disclose private information.

The station may include, subject to professional review:

- Clear information on overdose recognition and emergency response.
- Information on how to contact emergency services and the Swedish Poison Information Centre.
- Naloxone access and instructions consistent with applicable rules and training.
- Appropriate hygiene and protective supplies.
- First-aid information.
- Information on hydration and other general health considerations.
- Contact details for voluntary, nonjudgmental substance-use support.

The station must not be used to monitor visitors, record their substance use, or determine who deserves access.

## 5. Emergency equipment and life-saving preparedness

Refugium must plan for an emergency response system that includes appropriate equipment, training, maintenance, and clear responsibilities.

The proposed baseline includes:

- A well-stocked first-aid kit.
- CPR and resuscitation equipment appropriate to the setting.
- A suitable resuscitation mask and protective equipment.
- An accessible, clearly marked automated external defibrillator (AED), commonly called a heart-starting device.
- Naloxone access and appropriate training.
- A clear process for contacting 112.
- Written procedures for medical emergencies and handover to emergency personnel.
- Regular equipment checks, maintenance, replacement, and documented responsibility for readiness.

The organization must assess the actual building, staffing, opening hours, accessibility, and visitor needs to determine the precise equipment and placement.

Where Refugium operates around the clock, emergency preparedness must also work at night and during periods of reduced staffing.

A device that is locked away, unmaintained, inaccessible, or unknown to staff does not provide reliable preparedness.

## 6. Medication, activated charcoal, and supplements

The presence of emergency equipment does not authorize Witnesses to diagnose or treat medical conditions beyond their competence.

Naloxone, activated charcoal, other medicines, and supplements must be handled according to professionally reviewed procedures and applicable Swedish requirements.

Activated charcoal must not be administered routinely or on a visitor's request without appropriate medical guidance. It can be unsuitable or dangerous in some circumstances.

Supplements such as magnesium or electrolyte products must not be treated as general antidotes to substance-related symptoms.

Muscle cramps, overheating, chest pain, seizures, severe confusion, abnormal breathing, or collapse after substance use may indicate a serious medical condition.

When serious symptoms occur, call 112 as appropriate and follow professional instructions rather than attempting to manage the situation with supplements.

## 7. Emergency training

Witnesses must receive training appropriate to their responsibilities in:

- Recognizing possible overdose and serious intoxication.
- Recognizing abnormal breathing and cardiac arrest.
- CPR and use of an AED.
- Naloxone use, where applicable.
- Recognizing signs that require urgent medical assistance.
- Contacting 112 and following dispatcher instructions.
- Communicating relevant information to emergency personnel.
- Protecting privacy during and after an emergency.
- Managing the emotional impact of serious incidents.

The organization must determine and maintain appropriate refresher training.

## 8. No moralized response

Substance use must never be used as a shortcut for judging credibility, worth, deservingness, or access to the Baseline.

A visitor must not be required to become abstinent before receiving ordinary care.

Refugium should offer information and external support when wanted, without coercion.

**The purpose is not to guarantee a risk-free life. The purpose is to reduce preventable harm without abandoning the people who may be affected by it.**

# VII. Boundaries, confidentiality, and ethics

## 1. Conflicts of interest and dual relationships

Witnessing involves a position of responsibility and trust.

Personal relationships can create conflicts of interest, especially when the Witness has influence over access, safety decisions, complaints, or support.

The previous absolute prohibition on any prior relationship is replaced with a conflict-of-interest procedure.

A person who has a relevant personal, family, romantic, financial, or other close relationship with a visitor must disclose the conflict through an appropriate confidential process.

Depending on the situation, the person may need to:

- Decline the assignment.
- Arrange another Witness.
- Recuse themselves from decisions affecting the visitor.
- Seek supervision or guidance.

Prior acquaintance does not automatically make witnessing impossible, particularly in a small community. However, the organization must prevent exploitation, dependency, favoritism, retaliation, and inappropriate use of confidential information.

No Witness may use their role to initiate or exploit a romantic, sexual, financial, or coercive relationship with a visitor.

The precise policy must be professionally reviewed and adapted to the role and applicable law.

## 2. Confidentiality

Visitors should be able to use Refugium without routine registration of their identity, reasons for visiting, or frequency of visits.

Witnesses must not gossip about visitors or share their stories for personal interest, entertainment, or social status.

Confidentiality is a foundational expectation, but it must not be promised as absolute.

Limited disclosure may be necessary where legally required or where a concrete and serious safety concern requires appropriate action.

Before operational use, the organization must define and legally review:

- Applicable confidentiality and data-protection obligations.
- Child-protection reporting requirements.
- Emergency disclosure procedures.
- Any applicable legal obligations concerning court orders or other lawful demands.
- Who may receive information and for what purpose.

Witnesses should explain relevant limits in plain language whenever practicable.

## 3. Touch and physical assistance

Consent is the default for touch.

Witnesses must not assume that touch is comforting, welcome, or necessary.

Ask before offering a hug, holding someone's hand, or providing other non-urgent physical contact.

Physical assistance, transfers, and emergency first aid require additional attention to competence, safety, and the person's wishes.

A rule against unnecessary touch must not prevent necessary assistance in an emergency. Conversely, an emergency rationale must not become a blanket justification for intrusive contact.

Children's needs, communication, safeguarding, and physical assistance must be addressed through the appropriate child-specific procedures.

## 4. Power and accountability

Witnesses must recognize the power that comes with access to private spaces, information, and responsibility for safety.

Visitors must be able to:

- Question a decision.
- Ask for another Witness.
- Decline nonessential assistance.
- Raise concerns about treatment.
- Make a complaint.
- Seek review of a decision without fear of retaliation.

Serious allegations must not be handled solely by the person accused.

The organization must provide a clear route for independent review and appropriate protection against retaliation.

No Witness or manager has personal ownership over Refugium or its visitors.

# VIII. Sustainability, supervision, and the Witness's own needs

## 1. Sustainable workload

The organization must provide staffing that protects both visitors and Witnesses.

The previous universal workload limits are retained as a subject for operational planning, not as validated universal rules.

Workload must be based on:

- The nature and intensity of the role.
- Staffing levels and shift arrangements.
- Breaks and recovery time.
- The physical and emotional demands of the environment.
- Applicable employment and workplace-safety requirements.
- Individual capacity and reasonable adjustments.

The organization must not rely on unpaid or under-supported personal sacrifice to keep a 24-hour sanctuary open.

No Witness should be expected to conceal exhaustion or compromise their health to avoid disappointing the organization.

## 2. Supervision and debriefing

Regular support must be available to Witnesses.

The organization should distinguish between:

- **Peer debriefing:** mutual support and practical reflection.
- **Professional supervision:** structured guidance by an appropriately qualified person.
- **Personal therapy or healthcare:** individual support outside the operational supervision role.

These functions are not interchangeable.

Debriefing must respect visitors' privacy and must not become a setting for sharing identifiable stories without a legitimate, authorized purpose.

Witnesses must be able to report organizational problems, request relief, and pause duties when necessary without shame or retaliation.

## 3. Secondary traumatic stress and overload

Witnesses may experience exhaustion, intrusive thoughts, emotional numbing, irritability, or difficulty recovering after demanding situations.

These signs should be taken seriously without using a simple symptom count as an automatic diagnosis or disciplinary threshold.

The appropriate response may include:

- Speaking with a designated responsible person.
- Reviewing workload and current capacity.
- Requesting a break or relief.
- Seeking supervision.
- Arranging an appropriate handover.
- Accessing personal support or healthcare if wanted or needed.

The organization must not assume that every difficult reaction is a personal failure.

## 4. The Witness's own sanctuary access

Witnesses retain the right to rest, seek support, and use Refugium according to the same foundational principles as other people, with reasonable arrangements for role conflicts and privacy.

Needing help does not disqualify someone from witnessing.

A Witness who is off duty must not be pressured to continue working merely because they are present in the building.

Witnesses must be able to seek support from another person rather than being expected to provide care for themselves while maintaining their professional role.

# IX. Training pathways and competence review

## 1. Proposed base training

The original training plan proposed 60 hours:

- Philosophy and role: 12 hours.
- Presence, observation, and regulation: 16 hours.
- Ethics and boundaries: 12 hours.
- Crisis response and emergency procedures: 10 hours.
- Supervised practical work: 10 hours.

These hours are a planning framework, not proof of competence.

The final curriculum must be reviewed to ensure that the allocated time is appropriate for the responsibilities and that practical skills are actually assessed.

Training must cover the principles and procedures in this document, including:

- Autonomy and the Silent Exit Protocol.
- Non-interpretation.
- Accessible communication.
- Observation without diagnosis.
- Consent and boundaries.
- Confidentiality and privacy.
- Conflict-of-interest management.
- Child safeguarding where relevant.
- Emergency recognition and escalation.
- Substance-use harm reduction.
- First aid, CPR, and AED use appropriate to the role.
- Incident documentation and handover.
- Complaints and organizational accountability.
- Recognition of personal limits and the right to request relief.

Any nervous-system or psychological material must be presented accurately, with a distinction between established professional knowledge, practical guidance, and unverified explanatory claims.

## 2. Practical assessment

Competence must be assessed through practical scenarios, discussion, observed performance, and appropriate supervision.

Assessment should consider whether a trainee can:

- Respect a request to leave.
- Offer assistance without imposing it.
- Identify a concrete safety concern without overinterpreting behavior.
- Ask relevant questions in a medical emergency.
- Follow emergency instructions.
- Maintain boundaries without punishment.
- Protect confidentiality.
- Recognize when to seek help.
- Handle complaints and conflicts of interest appropriately.

Assessment must not reward conformity to a personal ideology or demand that trainees adopt a particular interpretation of visitors' experiences.

## 3. Child-specialist pathway

The additional child-specific pathway must include appropriate competence in:

- Child development.
- Safeguarding and reporting.
- Accessible communication.
- Boundaries and physical assistance.
- Non-directive creative support.
- Responding to disclosures.
- Recognizing when specialist help is needed.
- Practical scenarios and supervised practice.

Training must not be represented as a substitute for professional qualifications required for particular tasks.

## 4. Continuing development

The original proposal included annual continuing development of:

- 12 hours of supervision.
- 6 hours of ethics training.
- 4 hours of crisis-procedure updates.

These figures should be reviewed and adopted only if appropriate to the role and applicable requirements.

Emergency procedures and equipment-specific training must be refreshed as needed.

Competence should be reviewed periodically and after relevant incidents, significant procedural changes, or prolonged absence from the role.

A person must be able to seek additional training without being stigmatized.

# X. Examples of witnessing

These scenarios illustrate principles rather than prescribe identical responses in every situation.

## 1. An adult is crying in a rest room

**Appropriate response**

The Witness checks whether contact is wanted, using the least intrusive approach consistent with the situation.

If the visitor asks to be alone, the Witness leaves unless a concrete immediate danger requires a different response.

The Witness does not demand an explanation, interpret the crying, or repeatedly return simply because the visitor has not stopped crying.

If the visitor requests help, the Witness responds to the request.

If a concrete medical emergency becomes apparent, the Witness follows the appropriate emergency procedure.

## 2. A child is painting

**Appropriate response**

The Witness makes materials available and allows the child to direct their own activity.

The Witness does not interpret the picture, demand an explanation, or assume that the painting reveals a psychological condition.

The child may stop or change activities.

If the child expresses a safeguarding concern, the Witness listens without interrogation and follows the appropriate procedure.

## 3. A visitor uses the scream and impact room

**Appropriate response**

The Witness respects the visitor's privacy while remaining available according to the room's safety arrangements.

The Witness does not enter solely because a predetermined duration has passed.

If the visitor requests assistance, there is a credible sign of injury, or a concrete emergency arises, the Witness responds proportionately and seeks additional help when needed.

## 4. A visitor arrives under the influence of a substance

**Appropriate response**

The visitor is not automatically denied access or subjected to a drug test.

The Witness responds to the person as an individual, offers assistance if appropriate, and does not assume that intoxication necessarily means danger.

If the visitor develops concerning symptoms, the Witness asks relevant questions where possible and seeks medical assistance according to the emergency procedure.

If concrete behavior threatens another visitor, the Witness addresses that behavior and protects those affected without turning substance use itself into a moral judgment.

## 5. A visitor asks the Witness to leave

**Appropriate response**

The Witness acknowledges the request and leaves without demanding an explanation.

If there is a concrete immediate danger, the Witness follows the relevant safety procedure, using the least intrusive proportionate response.

The request is not treated as hostility, rejection, or an insult.

## 6. A visitor complains about a Witness

**Appropriate response**

The visitor is given information about an accessible complaint pathway.

They are not required to confront the Witness directly.

The complaint is referred through the designated process, with privacy protections and safeguards against retaliation.

The person complained about must not be the sole decision-maker concerning the complaint.

## 7. A Witness realizes they cannot continue safely

**Appropriate response**

The Witness informs the designated responsible person and requests relief.

Where a visitor needs ongoing support, the organization arranges a handover.

The Witness is not shamed for having limits, and the organization does not treat staffing failures as the individual's personal responsibility.

# XI. Complaints, documentation, and organizational accountability

## 1. Accessible complaints

Refugium must provide clear and accessible ways for visitors to report:

- Boundary violations.
- Discrimination.
- Harassment.
- Unsafe conditions.
- Misuse of authority.
- Confidentiality breaches.
- Inappropriate treatment.
- Other concerns about the operation.

Visitors must not be required to complain directly to the person concerned.

The process should allow anonymous complaints where practicable, while explaining the limitations this may create for investigation and follow-up.

Serious allegations must be reviewed by an appropriately independent person or process.

Retaliation against complainants, witnesses, or people seeking review is prohibited.

## 2. Minimal documentation and privacy

Refugium should not routinely register who visits, why they come, how often they visit, or how they appear emotionally.

Ordinary use of the sanctuary should not automatically create a personal file.

Documentation may be necessary for certain serious incidents, safety reviews, legal obligations, or organizational learning.

When documentation is justified, it must be:

- Limited to relevant facts.
- Proportionate to the purpose.
- Restricted to authorized recipients.
- Stored and protected appropriately.
- Retained only for a justified period.
- Reviewed under the applicable data-protection and legal framework.

Distinguish observed facts from assumptions, diagnoses, and unverified accounts.

Incident documentation must never become a hidden scoring system for deciding whether someone deserves access.

The exact procedure must be legally reviewed before implementation.

## 3. Organizational responsibility

The local node and the responsible organization must carry responsibility for:

- Staffing and coverage.
- Training and competence review.
- Physical safety and accessibility.
- Equipment and maintenance.
- Emergency preparedness.
- Workload and workplace safety.
- Complaint handling.
- Privacy and information governance.
- Policy review and incident learning.
- Access to appropriate professional advice.

Witnesses must not be expected to compensate indefinitely for missing resources or defective organizational procedures.

The organization must make responsibilities clear, including who can make operational decisions and how those decisions can be challenged.

No local node or individual owns the visitors or has unrestricted authority to revoke access to the Baseline.

Any necessary restriction must be grounded in a concrete and proportionate reason, subject to appropriate review and consistent with applicable law.

## 4. Transparency and prevention of person-centred authority

Refugium must not become dependent on the charisma, personal beliefs, or unchallengeable authority of an individual.

Visitors must not be expected to express gratitude, follow a doctrine, participate in a community, or obey a particular person to receive care.

Rules, responsibilities, and complaint routes must be understandable and accessible.

Witnesses and managers must be accountable for their decisions.

Expertise may improve understanding and safety. It must not become a shortcut around the visitor's dignity, legitimate autonomy, or the organization's accountability.

# XII. Physical safety and everyday operations

Refugium must maintain a separate, practical safety plan appropriate to the actual premises.

The plan should address:

- Fire prevention and evacuation.
- Accessible emergency exits.
- First aid, CPR, and AED readiness.
- Emergency communications and escalation.
- Inspection of rooms and equipment.
- Safe use of the scream and impact room.
- Water facilities, bathing, and transfers.
- Food hygiene, drinking water, and sanitation.
- Infection prevention where relevant.
- Accessibility for people with different mobility, sensory, and communication needs.
- Animals and other site-specific considerations.
- Safe storage and handling of medical supplies.
- Procedures for incidents involving substances.
- Staffing and emergency readiness during all operating hours.

Alcohol or other substance use must not automatically disqualify a person from entering.

Rules must address concrete risks, behavior, and the safety of people sharing the environment, rather than relying on blanket moral judgments.

The organization must establish appropriate procedures for situations in which a person's needs exceed the available facilities or competence. Such procedures must not become automatic exclusion simply because a situation is inconvenient.

# XIII. Professional and legal review before implementation

The following areas require appropriate review under Swedish law and professional standards.

## Legal review

- Child safeguarding, reporting duties, and disclosures.
- Confidentiality, data protection, incident records, and retention.
- Emergency response and relevant legal responsibilities.
- Organizational and employment responsibilities.
- Workplace safety, staffing, and working hours.
- Accessibility and applicable discrimination requirements.
- Conflicts of interest and professional boundaries.
- Storage, access, and administration of medicines and medical supplies.
- Complaints, independent review, and protection against retaliation.

## Medical and professional review

- Recognition of medical emergencies and appropriate escalation.
- Suicide-risk procedures and handover to healthcare or emergency services.
- Overdose response and harm-reduction procedures.
- Naloxone availability, training, and use.
- CPR, AED placement, and first-aid training.
- Activated charcoal and other medicines or supplements.
- Rest and sleep safety.
- Transfers, bathing, falls, and physical assistance.
- Child development, safeguarding, and communication.
- Safe design and inspection of specialist rooms.
- Fire safety, evacuation, and accessible emergency arrangements.

This list identifies matters requiring review. It does not itself determine which legal duties apply to every possible organizational model.

The final procedures must be adapted to the actual premises, staffing arrangements, services offered, and legal status of the organization.

# XIV. Final principles

The Witness is not here to make another person into a success story.

The Witness is not here to demand understanding, gratitude, disclosure, improvement, or recovery.

The Witness is here to listen, ask when appropriate, respect boundaries, and help protect the conditions in which a person may exist without being required to justify their existence.

The Witness does not own the visitor's experience.

The Witness does not determine what the visitor's story means.

The Witness does not have to be invisible. The Witness must avoid making themselves the centre of the experience while remaining available, visible when needed, and accountable for their actions.

The Witness must be able to recognize the limits of their competence and call for appropriate assistance.

The organization must provide the resources, staffing, training, and accountability necessary for the Witness role to function safely.

No person loses their fundamental dignity because they are distressed, silent, angry, disabled, intoxicated, uncertain, or unable to explain what they need.

No person should have to earn the right to receive care by becoming easier to care for.

**Skadereducering är en del av tryggheten för alla. Baslinjen villkoras inte av drogfrihet. Säkerhet skapas genom tillgänglig hjälp, kunskap, fungerande beredskap och respekt för varje människas värdighet.**

Refugium must not promise that nothing difficult will ever happen.

It must take responsibility for how it responds when something difficult happens.

The purpose is to protect autonomy without abandoning people, to establish boundaries without punishment, and to make care possible without making it conditional on conformity.

**Witnessing is presence without demand, combined with responsibility without ownership.**
