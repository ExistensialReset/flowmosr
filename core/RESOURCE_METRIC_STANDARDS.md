**Status:** GLOBAL BASELINE STANDARD  
**Scope:** Planetary

# PURPOSE

This document defines the **Global Baseline Resource Floor per Individual**.

Baseline is not charity.  
Baseline is not welfare.  
Baseline is not political.

Baseline is the minimum biological and cognitive stability required for a human being to function without structural coercion.

Baseline is a **floor**, never a ceiling.

No Node, State, Collective, or System may go below this floor.

---

# I. CORE PRINCIPLES

1. Defined per individual.
2. Expressed in measurable SI units where applicable.
3. Climate-independent at the biological level.
4. Immutable without global LOTUS majority.
5. Cannot be revoked as punishment.

## BASELINE ACCESS PRINCIPLE

Baseline guarantees access to the conditions required for life, autonomy, and dignity.

Baseline does not require consumption.

A person may choose:

- solitude
- social participation
- travel or staying home
- sweets or no sweets
- medication or no medication where medically appropriate
- substance use or non-use
- work or rest within the limits of collective responsibility

The right is the right to access, not the obligation to use.

---

# II. BIOLOGICAL ENERGY & NUTRITION

Baseline must provide sufficient energy and nutrition for the individual to maintain physiological health.

## Reference Energy Floor

For adults, a reference range of approximately **25–30 kcal per kg body weight per day** may be used as a starting point.

Actual individual requirements must be adjusted for:

- age
- body composition
- physical activity
- illness
- pregnancy and lactation
- climate
- metabolic variation
- other physiological needs

No person may be systematically deprived of the energy or nutrients required for their actual physiological needs.

### Children and Adolescents

Energy requirements for children and adolescents must be adjusted for:

- age
- body weight
- growth
- physical activity
- developmental stage
- illness
- other physiological needs

### Macronutrients

- Protein: approximately **0.8 g per kg body weight per day** as a general adult reference, with higher requirements where appropriate for growth, activity, pregnancy, illness, or other physiological needs.
- Fat: approximately **20–35% of total caloric intake** as a general reference range.
- Carbohydrates: remaining caloric requirements according to individual physiological needs.

### Micronutrients

Nutritional provision should meet relevant international health standards, including WHO recommendations where applicable.

Diet must remain flexible enough to accommodate:

- cultural variation
- regional food systems
- individual preference
- allergies
- medical requirements
- ethical choices

---

# III. WATER

## Drinking Water

- Minimum: **3 liters per person per day**, subject to individual physiological and climatic needs.

## Total Water Access

- Minimum reference allocation: **100 liters per person per day**, including hygiene and sanitation.

Water must meet appropriate potable-water safety standards.

Water access must also account for:

- disability
- medical needs
- climate
- local infrastructure
- hygiene requirements
- household and community conditions

No person may be systematically deprived of the water required for physiological health, hygiene, sanitation, and dignity.

---

# IV. LIFE QUALITY, JOY & CULTURAL ACCESS

Baseline is not limited to bare biological survival.

Every person should have meaningful access to:

- flavor and culinary diversity
- cultural expression
- play
- art
- music
- celebration
- beauty
- wonder

These are not luxuries in the sense of being irrelevant to human life.

However, access does not imply mandatory participation or equal allocation of every form of pleasure.

A society that guarantees survival while systematically eliminating joy, beauty, play, and cultural life has fulfilled biological survival but not the broader purpose of Flow.

---

# V. AIR QUALITY

Baseline environments should provide:

- Indoor CO₂ concentration: preferably below **1,000 ppm**
- PM2.5: maximum target of **15 µg/m³ annual average** where achievable
- Air exchange rate: minimum reference of **0.5 ACH** where applicable

Standards must be interpreted according to:

- climate
- building type
- local environmental conditions
- medical vulnerability
- available infrastructure

No person may be systematically subjected to air conditions that create significant risk of illness or physiological harm.

---

# VI. TEMPERATURE & CLIMATE STABILITY

## Reference Thermal Range

- Habitable indoor temperature target: **18–24°C**
- Relative humidity target: **30–60%**

These ranges are reference targets for thermal comfort and physiological stability.

No individual may be subjected to prolonged thermal conditions that create significant risk of:

- illness
- injury
- heat stress
- hypothermia
- inability to maintain physiological safety

In diverse climates, local adaptation may be necessary.

Voluntary preference to live outside reference ranges must not be interpreted as consent to unsafe conditions.

---

# VII. ELECTRICAL ENERGY

Energy access has two separate dimensions.

## A. Continuous Available Capacity

- Minimum available capacity: **1,000 W per person**
- Capacity may be pooled at Node level where appropriate.
- Additional capacity must be provided where required by medical equipment or accessibility needs.

## B. Annual Energy Allocation

- Minimum annual energy allocation or availability: **2,000 kWh per person per year**

These are separate metrics.

**Continuous capacity** concerns how much power can be supplied at a given moment.

**Annual allocation** concerns the total amount of energy available over time.

Neither metric may be used to justify coercive control over an individual's legitimate use of Baseline resources.

---

# VIII. LIGHT

Baseline environments should provide:

- approximately **300 lux** at working surfaces
- approximately **100 lux** ambient lighting
- access to stable light for approximately **8 hours daily**, through natural or artificial sources as appropriate

Lighting must be adaptable to:

- disability
- sensory needs
- sleep requirements
- work and education
- local climate and architecture

---

# IX. HABITABLE SPACE

- Absolute minimum reference: **15 m² per individual**
- Ceiling height must allow normal upright movement.
- Space must provide access to:
  - sleeping
  - personal storage
  - sanitation
  - food preparation

Habitable space must also account for:

- mobility aids
- accessibility requirements
- medical equipment
- privacy
- household composition
- shared and communal infrastructure

No person may be systematically confined to living conditions that prevent basic dignity, safety, mobility, or bodily autonomy.

---

# X. TIME AS A RESOURCE

Baseline includes protected time.

Every person has a right to:

- sufficient sleep
- protected autonomous time
- freedom from chronic time extraction

As a reference framework:

- approximately **8 hours of sleep opportunity**
- maximum **6 hours of system-mandated labor or contribution per day**
- minimum **2 hours of autonomous discretionary time**

The 6-hour limit applies to compulsory system-mandated contribution.

Voluntary activity is not limited by this clause.

No system may structurally require chronic extraction of time beyond the Baseline threshold.

---

# XI. ACCESS TO INFORMATION & CONNECTIVITY

Every person has the right to access:

- functional internet or equivalent knowledge networks
- educational materials
- communication infrastructure
- tools necessary for mutual visibility and participation

Information deprivation is structural deprivation.

No system may deliberately isolate individuals from essential knowledge or communication infrastructure as a method of coercion or control.

---

# XII. MOBILITY, EXPLORATION & WORLD ACCESS

Every person has the right to access experiences beyond their immediate living environment.

For children and young people, this includes meaningful opportunities to:

- experience different regions, environments, or cultures
- travel beyond their immediate local area
- encounter people and ways of living different from their own

As a reference minimum, every individual aged **7–25** should have access to at least **two longer journeys** during this developmental period, where desired and appropriate.

Such journeys may include:

- train travel
- boat travel
- equivalent forms of accessible transportation

Such opportunities must be:

- accessible
- safe
- age-appropriate
- culturally respectful
- compatible with individual consent and preference

No person is required to travel.

The right is a right to access and opportunity, not a requirement to participate.

## Transportation

Mass transit should be:

- accessible
- available
- safe
- affordable or free where possible

Mobility supports:

- learning
- autonomy
- social participation
- exposure to diversity
- broader understanding of the world

---

# XIII. SOCIAL ACCESS & NON-ISOLATION

Every person has the right to access:

- communication tools
- communal spaces
- cultural spaces
- educational spaces
- social environments
- the ability to leave and re-enter communities freely

Social access is a right of availability, not a requirement of participation.

No person may be:

- forcibly isolated
- systematically excluded
- denied communication
- required to maintain a prescribed level of social interaction as a condition of Baseline access

Rest, solitude, privacy, and non-participation are protected forms of autonomy.

---

# XIV. BIOCHEMICAL SOVEREIGNTY

Access to information, harm reduction, healthcare, and bodily and cognitive autonomy regarding substance use is a Baseline right.

Adults have the right to:

- evidence-based information
- harm-reduction tools
- healthcare without criminalization
- voluntary access to regulated and tested substances where appropriate under applicable safety frameworks

Where a substance is socially demanded, prohibition should not automatically be assumed to be the safest policy.

The system must evaluate whether:

- regulated production
- purity control
- education
- transparent analysis
- healthcare
- harm reduction

can reduce the harms created by illegal markets.

Different substances require different safety frameworks.

Cannabis, psychedelics, stimulants, medicines, and other substances should not automatically be treated as identical.

Purity and safety should be ensured through transparent local analysis where possible.

Care over control: substance use is a health and sovereignty issue, not a moral failure.

Necessary medical substances must remain accessible to those who require them.

Prolonged restriction of medically necessary substances must trigger intervention.

## Core Principle

> If demand exists, prohibition does not make the demand disappear.
>
> It may instead transfer production and distribution to systems with less transparency, less quality control, and greater risk of violence and exploitation.

The question is therefore not simply:

> “How do we prevent people from using substances?”

but:

> “How do we reduce harm, protect cognitive and bodily sovereignty, and prevent illegal markets from becoming the primary source of substances that people will seek regardless of prohibition?”

---

# XV. VERIFICATION WITHOUT SURVEILLANCE

Baseline verification exists to identify structural deprivation and resource failure.

Verification may include:

1. Self-reporting
2. Voluntary peer or community review
3. Aggregated environmental or infrastructure data
4. Independent audit where structural failure is disputed
5. LOTUS review in cases of unresolved systemic dispute

Verification must never become continuous surveillance of individual behavior.

No person is required to prove their:

- productivity
- emotional state
- social activity
- substance use
- consumption patterns
- personal compliance

in order to retain Baseline access.

Aggregated data may be used to identify systemic deprivation.

Individual transparency is voluntary except where narrowly defined protections against direct harm require otherwise.

---

# XVI. NON-REVOCABILITY CLAUSE

Baseline cannot be:

- suspended as punishment
- withheld for debt
- traded for compliance
- reduced through ordinary majority vote

Any attempt to weaponize Baseline invalidates governance authority.

No Node, State, Collective, or System may deliberately remove Baseline access as a method of coercion.

---

# XVII. REVISION PROTOCOL

Baseline may only be changed through:

- Global LOTUS Majority of at least **66%**
- public amendment window
- open verification audit
- transparent publication of proposed changes

No revision may reduce the Baseline floor merely to increase productivity, profit, political control, or resource extraction.

---

# XVIII. INTERPRETATION & FLEXIBILITY IN DIVERSE CLIMATES

The numerical standards in this document are global reference minima.

They are designed to provide a common planetary floor while allowing adaptation to:

- climate
- infrastructure
- culture
- local ecology
- accessibility
- individual physiological needs

Local adaptation must not be used to justify structural deprivation.

## Temperature & Humidity

The reference range of **18–24°C** and **30–60% relative humidity** represents a target for thermal safety and comfort.

In extreme climates, achieving these ranges may require significant energy.

A person may voluntarily prefer to live outside these reference ranges, provided that:

- the environment does not create significant physiological risk
- alternatives are reasonably available where necessary
- consent is reversible
- no person is coerced into unsafe conditions

## Energy

The **2,000 kWh per year** figure represents minimum annual energy availability or allocation.

It is separate from the **1,000 W continuous available capacity** standard.

Nodes are encouraged to exceed both standards whenever possible.

## Mandatory Contribution

The **6-hour daily maximum** applies to system-mandated labor or contribution.

Voluntary work, creativity, care, learning, and contribution are not limited by this clause.

All adaptations must be transparent, documented where appropriate, and reviewable by the Node Circle.

If a system cannot guarantee Baseline, it has no moral or structural legitimacy.

Baseline is the first invariant of Flow.
